# 1. Drug-Coated Balloons (DCB) / Drug-Eluting Balloons

## 1.1 Clinical Background and Guidelines

Drug-coated balloons deliver antiproliferative drugs (paclitaxel or sirolimus) to the vessel wall without leaving a permanent implant. The 2024 ESC Guidelines for Chronic Coronary Syndromes now recommend DES over DCB for DES-ISR (Class IA), though DCB remains preferred for:
- Patients with multiple stent layers
- High bleeding risk patients
- BMS-ISR lesions
- Small vessel disease
- Bifurcation lesions

## 1.2 Paclitaxel vs. Sirolimus Comparison

| Parameter | Paclitaxel DCB | Sirolimus DCB |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| **Mechanism** | Cytotoxic - microtubule interference | Cytostatic - mTOR inhibition |
| **Therapeutic Range** | Narrow | Wide |
| **Drug Transfer** | High lipophilicity, easier delivery | Requires specialized coating technology |
| **Dose Density** | 2-3.5 μg/mm² | 1.27-4 μg/mm² |
| **Safety Profile** | Some late safety concerns (mortality signal in meta-analyses for peripheral) | Favorable safety with sustained antiproliferative effect |
| **Clinical Evidence** | Extensive (>15 years) | Growing (newer generation) |

### Key Clinical Finding:
The SCB vs PCB in ISR trial showed sirolimus-coated balloon angioplasty was **non-inferior** to paclitaxel-coated balloon angioplasty, with identical late lumen loss at 6 months (0.30 mm in both groups).

# 2. Cutting Balloons

## 2.1 Clinical Background

Cutting balloons use microsurgical blades (atherotomes) to create controlled incisions in plaque, allowing more effective lesion expansion with less barotrauma. They are particularly valuable for:
- Severely calcified lesions
- In-stent restenosis
- Ostial lesions
- Lesions resistant to standard balloon dilation

## 2.2 Top Cutting Balloon Products

### 2.2.1 WOLVERINE Cutting Balloon (Boston Scientific) - **ONLY FDA-APPROVED OPTION**

| Parameter | Data |
|-----------|------|
| **Design** | Atherotome blades creating micro-incisions |
| **Profile** | Lower than predecessor (Flextome) |
| **Coating** | Lubricious coating on distal segment |
| **Key Evidence** | >30 years of clinical use; CUPID trial ongoing |
| **Perforation Risk** | OR 0.62 (95% CI 0.43-0.89) - *Lower than POBA* |
| **vs Scoring Balloon** | Superior MSA at calcium site in RCT |
| **FDA Status** | Only FDA-approved coronary cutting balloon |
| **CE Mark** | Yes |
| **Price Tier** | Premium |

**Clinical Evidence:**
- UK registry (>500,000 PCIs): Cutting balloons associated with **lower perforation risk** vs no cutting balloon (OR 0.62)
- Meta-analysis: Cutting balloon reduces MACE and TLR vs NC balloon in calcified lesions
- Superior minimal stent area (MSA) at calcium site vs POBA

**Advantages:**
- Only FDA-approved device
- Proven safety profile (lower perforation rate)
- Superior calcium modification vs scoring balloons
- Improved crossability vs older Flextome model

**Limitations:**
- Premium pricing
- Limited competition (near-monopoly)
- Crossability still inferior to some scoring balloons in certain lesions

### 2.2.2 Flextome (Boston Scientific) - **LEGACY PRODUCT**

## 2.3 Alternatives and Availability

**Chinese/Indian Alternatives:** Limited comparable products with clinical evidence. No cutting balloons from Chinese or Indian manufacturers have demonstrated equivalent clinical outcomes to the Wolverine.

**Recommendation:** For cutting balloon capability, the Wolverine remains the standard of care with no evidence-based alternatives from Asian manufacturers.
