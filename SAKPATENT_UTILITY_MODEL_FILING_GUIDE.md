## About Sakpatent

**SAKPATENTI** (საქპატენტი) is the **National Intellectual Property Center of Georgia**, the country's official patent office responsible for registering and protecting intellectual property rights including:
- Patents for inventions
- Utility models (სასარგებლო მოდელი)
- Industrial designs
- Trademarks
- Geographical indications
- Copyright

### Official Information
- **Official Website:** https://www.sakpatenti.gov.ge/en/ (English) / https://www.sakpatenti.gov.ge/ka/ (Georgian)
- **Online Filing Portal:** https://online.sakpatenti.gov.ge/
- **Member of:** WIPO (World Intellectual Property Organization), PCT (Patent Cooperation Treaty since 1991)

## What is a Utility Model (სასარგებლო მოდელი)?

### Definition
A **utility model** is a form of intellectual property protection similar to a patent but designed for incremental innovations, improvements, and adaptations of existing technologies. In Georgia, utility models provide legal protection for technical solutions.

### Key Characteristics in Georgia
- **Protection Term:** 10 years from filing date
- **Novelty Grace Period:** 12 months (you can disclose your invention up to 12 months before filing without losing protection rights)
- **Subject Matter:** Same as patents - products (devices, processes, substances, microorganism strains, cell cultures) and methods
- **Claims Requirement:** Must have only ONE independent claim
- **Examination:** Local novelty check (less stringent than patent examination)
- **Timeline:** Approximately 1 year for complete registration

### What Can Be Protected as a Utility Model?
According to Georgian legislation, a utility model application may cover:
- **Products:** Devices, apparatus, instruments
- **Processes:** Manufacturing methods, procedures
- **Substances:** Chemical compounds, materials
- **Biological materials:** Strains of microorganisms, animal and plant cell cultures
- **Methods:** New ways of using existing products

**Important:** The application must relate to ONE utility model with one independent claim.

## Utility Model vs Patent - Which to Choose?

### Quick Comparison Table

| Feature | Utility Model (სასარგებლო მოდელი) | Patent (პატენტი) |
|---------|-----------------------------------|------------------|
| **Protection Duration** | 10 years | 20 years (extendable to 25 for pharma) |
| **Registration Time** | ~1 year | Longer (typically 1.5-3 years) |
| **Examination Type** | Local novelty only | Full novelty + inventive step |
| **Examination Duration** | 1 month after formal exam | 6 months after formal exam |
| **Claims Structure** | 1 independent claim | Multiple independent claims allowed |
| **Cost** | Lower fees | Higher fees |
| **Novelty Grace Period** | 12 months | 12 months |
| **Suitable For** | Incremental improvements, adaptations | Major innovations, breakthroughs |
| **Examination Fees** | $150 (up to 10 claims) | $300 (up to 10 claims) |
| **Registration Fee** | $150 | $200 |

### When to Choose Utility Model for Your Device

**Choose Utility Model if:**
- Your device is an improvement or adaptation of existing technology
- You need faster protection (urgent market entry)
- You want lower costs
- Your invention is technically sound but may not meet the high inventive step requirement
- You need protection for 10 years or less
- Your device has a shorter product lifecycle
- You want to test the market before investing in full patent protection

**Choose Patent if:**
- Your device represents a major breakthrough or novel solution
- You need long-term protection (20 years)
- You plan to license internationally
- You need multiple independent claims to cover various aspects
- You have sufficient budget for higher fees
- Your device has a long product lifecycle (medical devices typically do)

### For a Transfemoral Device (InsideOut_TransFemoral)
Given that you're developing a medical device (transfemoral prosthetic/orthotic technology), consider:
- **Medical devices often qualify for patents** due to their innovative nature
- **However, utility models are excellent for:**
  - Specific component improvements
  - Manufacturing method innovations
  - Attachment mechanisms
  - Adjustment systems
  - Comfort or fit enhancements

**Strategy Recommendation:** You could file a utility model first for faster protection while preparing a more comprehensive patent application, or file both if your device has multiple innovative aspects.

## Filing Options

You have several options for filing IP protection with Sakpatent:

### Option 1: Direct National Filing with Sakpatent
- **Timeline:** File directly with Sakpatent
- **Best for:** Protection only in Georgia
- **Advantage:** Fastest and most cost-effective for Georgian market
- **Process:** Use online e-filing system or paper application

### Option 2: PCT International Application
- **Timeline:** 31 months from priority date to enter Georgian national phase
- **Best for:** Multi-country protection including Georgia
- **Advantage:** Single application can designate Georgia + other countries
- **Fee Reduction:** 50% reduction on Sakpatent fees if ISA issued ISR and Written Opinion
- **Note:** Georgia has been a PCT member since 1991

### Option 3: Conventional (Paris Convention) Application
- **Timeline:** 12 months from priority date
- **Best for:** Claiming priority from earlier filing in another country
- **Process:** File in home country, then file in Georgia within 12 months

## Online Filing Process - Step by Step

### Step 1: Register for E-Filing Account

**Portal URL:** https://online.sakpatenti.gov.ge/

1. Visit the online portal
2. Click on **"რეგისტრაცია"** (Registration) button
3. Fill in registration form with:
   - Personal/Company information
   - Contact details
   - Email address (for notifications)
   - Create username and password
4. Verify your email address
5. Complete profile setup

**Benefits of Online Filing:**
- **20% discount** on all standard fees
- Faster processing
- Paperless proceedings with E-flow system
- Online tracking of application status
- Electronic notifications

### Step 2: Prepare Required Documents

**CRITICAL:** All forms must be filled out in **Georgian language**. Documents in foreign languages require certified Georgian translation within 2 months.

#### Documents to Prepare:
1. **Request Form (Application Form)** - in Georgian
2. **Description of the Utility Model** - detailed technical description
3. **Claims** - ONE independent claim (mandatory for utility models)
4. **Abstract** - brief summary (informational only)
5. **Drawings** (if necessary for understanding)
6. **Power of Attorney** (if using a representative) - simple signature, no legalization required initially
7. **Priority Document** (if claiming priority) - must submit within 3 months
8. **Assignment Deed** (if inventor and applicant are different)

**File Format Requirements:**
- Electronic files: .doc format
- Font: Sylfaen (Georgian font)
- Alternative: Paper application with electronic copy on CD-ROM

### Step 3: Log In to E-Filing Portal

1. Go to https://online.sakpatenti.gov.ge/
2. Click **"შესვლა"** (Login)
3. Enter your username and password
4. Access your dashboard

### Step 4: Start New Utility Model Application

1. Navigate to **Patents** section
2. Select **"სასარგებლო მოდელი"** (Utility Model)
3. Click **"ახალი განაცხადი"** (New Application)
4. Choose application type:
   - Direct national application
   - PCT national phase entry
   - Convention application (with priority)

### Step 5: Fill Out Online Application Form

Complete all sections (in Georgian):

**Section A: Applicant Information**
- Full name (individual) or company name
- Address
- Nationality
- Contact information (phone, email)

**Section B: Inventor Information**
- Full name of inventor(s)
- Address
- If inventor = applicant, indicate this

**Section C: Representative Information** (if applicable)
- Patent attorney name and registration number
- Or company representative details

**Section D: Priority Claim** (if applicable)
- Earlier application details
- Priority date
- Priority document reference

**Section E: Title of Utility Model**
- Clear, concise title describing the invention
- In Georgian

### Step 6: Upload Required Documents

Upload prepared documents in this order:
1. Description (PDF or .doc)
2. Claims (PDF or .doc)
3. Abstract (PDF or .doc)
4. Drawings (PDF, JPG, or TIFF)
5. Power of Attorney (scanned PDF)
6. Priority document (if applicable)
7. Other supporting documents

**Technical Requirements:**
- Maximum page limit: 30 pages (standard fee)
- Each additional page: $1 extra fee
- Ensure all Georgian text uses Sylfaen font

### Step 7: Review Application

1. Review all entered information carefully
2. Check uploaded documents for completeness
3. Verify claims structure (must have 1 independent claim)
4. Ensure all required fields are completed
5. Preview the complete application

### Step 8: Calculate and Pay Fees

**Online Fee Calculator:**
The system will automatically calculate fees based on:
- Number of pages (standard: up to 30 pages)
- Number of claims (standard: up to 10 claims)
- Additional services requested

**Payment Options:**
- Bank transfer
- Online payment (if available)
- Direct payment at Sakpatent office

**Discounts Available:**
- **20% discount for e-filing** (automatically applied)
- **70% discount for:** Applicant/inventors, higher educational institutions, independent scientific research units
- **90% discount for:** Students, school children, retired applicant/inventors

**Important:** After online payment, upload payment confirmation to your application.

### Step 9: Submit Application

1. Review final application summary
2. Accept terms and conditions
3. Click **"გაგზავნა"** (Submit)
4. Save the confirmation number and receipt
5. You will receive automatic email confirmation with application number

### Step 10: Track Your Application

1. Log in to your e-filing account
2. Go to **"ჩემი განაცხადები"** (My Applications)
3. Track status updates:
   - Received
   - Under formal examination
   - Under substantive examination
   - Decision pending
   - Granted/Rejected
4. Respond to any office actions through the portal

**Response Timeframes:**
- You must respond to Sakpatent notifications within specified deadlines (typically 2 months)
- Failure to respond results in proceedings termination

## Examination Process and Timeline

### Complete Timeline Overview

```
Filing → Formal Examination → Substantive Examination → Decision → Registration → Publication
  ↓            ↓                      ↓                    ↓            ↓            ↓
Day 0      2 weeks              1 month            1-2 weeks    1 month      Ongoing
```

**Total Estimated Time: ~1 year from filing to registration**

### Detailed Examination Stages

#### Stage 1: Receipt and Initial Processing (Day 0)
- Application received (online or paper)
- Application number assigned
- Filing date established
- Confirmation sent to applicant
- Application enters queue

**Action Required:** None. Wait for formal examination.

#### Stage 2: Formal Examination (Within 2 Weeks)

**What Sakpatent Checks:**
- [ ] Applicant identification and signature
- [ ] Representative documents (if applicable)
- [ ] Fee payment confirmation
- [ ] Completeness of application documents
- [ ] Correct format and language (Georgian)
- [ ] Compliance with formal requirements
- [ ] Unity of invention (single utility model)
- [ ] Claims structure (1 independent claim)

**Sakpatent Actions:**
- Assigns International Patent Classification (IPC) based on claims
- Reviews technical documentation completeness
- Issues formal examination report

**Possible Outcomes:**

**A. Application Accepted (Formal Examination Passed)**
- Notification sent to applicant
- Proceeds to substantive examination
- Invoice issued for substantive examination fee (if not paid)

**B. Formal Deficiencies Found**
- Office Action issued listing deficiencies
- Applicant must respond within 2 months
- Options:
  - Submit corrections/missing documents
  - Pay additional fees if applicable
  - Provide explanations
- After correction, proceeds to substantive examination

**C. Application Rejected (Formal Examination Failed)**
- If deficiencies not corrected within deadline
- Proceedings terminated
- Applicant may appeal or refile

**Action Required:**
- Pay substantive examination fee (if not already paid)
- Respond to any office actions within 2 months
- Submit any missing documents

#### Stage 3: Substantive Examination (1 Month After Formal Examination)

**Prerequisites:**
- Formal examination completed successfully
- Substantive examination fee paid

**What Sakpatent Examines:**

**1. Novelty (Local Novelty Check)**
- Searches Georgian patent databases
- Checks published utility models and patents in Georgia
- Reviews state of the art in Georgia
- Less stringent than patent examination (no worldwide novelty required)

**2. Industrial Applicability**
- Can the utility model be made or used in industry?
- Is it practically applicable?
- Does it solve a technical problem?

**3. Technical Requirements**
- Description completeness and clarity
- Claims properly define the scope
- Drawings adequately illustrate the invention
- Abstract accurately summarizes

**4. Unity of Invention**
- Confirms only one utility model in application
- Checks for single inventive concept

**Examination Duration:** 1 month (significantly faster than patents)

**Possible Outcomes:**

**A. Examination Passed - Grant Decision**
- Utility model meets all requirements
- Decision to Grant issued
- Invoice for registration fee sent
- Applicant has 1 month to pay registration fee

**B. Office Action - Issues Found**
- Examiner identifies issues:
  - Lack of novelty (prior art found in Georgia)
  - Unclear description or claims
  - Lack of industrial applicability
  - Technical deficiencies
- Office Action sent to applicant with details
- Applicant must respond within 2 months
- Options:
  - Amend claims or description
  - Provide arguments against examiner's findings
  - Submit additional evidence
  - Request interview with examiner

**C. Rejection**
- Fundamental issues cannot be overcome
- Application rejected
- Applicant may:
  - Appeal the decision
  - Request reconsideration with amendments
  - Abandon application

**Action Required:**
- Carefully review examination report
- Respond within 2 months to any office actions
- Amend claims if requested (must still be 1 independent claim)
- Provide technical arguments or evidence
- Consider consulting with patent attorney

#### Stage 4: Decision (1-2 Weeks)

**After Successful Examination:**
- Final review by senior examiner
- Decision to Grant issued formally
- Notification sent to applicant
- Registration fee invoice issued

**What the Decision Includes:**
- Confirmation of granted protection
- Approved claims (scope of protection)
- Patent/Utility Model number
- Instructions for registration payment

**Action Required:**
- Pay registration fee within 1 month
- Confirm acceptance of decision

#### Stage 5: Registration (Within 1 Month of Payment)

**After Registration Fee Payment:**
- Utility model officially registered
- Entry in State Register of Utility Models
- Certificate of Utility Model prepared
- Rights become enforceable

**What You Receive:**
- **Official Certificate** of Utility Model Registration
  - Certificate number
  - Registration date
  - Title of utility model
  - Applicant/owner name
  - Inventor name
  - Claims (scope of protection)
  - Official seal and signature

- **Registration Number** in format: U YYYY XXXX
  - U = Utility Model
  - YYYY = Year
  - XXXX = Sequential number

**Action Required:**
- Download certificate from e-filing portal
- Request official paper certificate (if desired)
- Pay for certified copies if needed

#### Stage 6: Publication (After Registration)

**Official Bulletin Publication:**
- Published in Sakpatent's Official Bulletin of Industrial Property
- Online publication on Sakpatent website
- Includes:
  - Bibliographic data
  - Abstract
  - Main drawing
  - Claims
  - Patent classification

**Publication Frequency:** Bulletins published regularly
**Access:** Free online at https://www.sakpatenti.gov.ge

**Public Notification:**
- Your utility model becomes public record
- Third parties can view registration details
- Important for establishing priority date

**Benefits of Publication:**
- Public notice of your rights
- Evidence of protection
- Can be used in enforcement actions
- Increases visibility of your innovation

## Patent Attorneys

### Why You Might Need a Patent Attorney

**Foreign Applicants:** If you are not a Georgian resident, you **MUST** be represented by a registered Georgian patent attorney.

**Optional for Georgian Residents:** You may represent yourself, but a patent attorney is recommended for:
- Complex technical inventions
- Responding to office actions
- Navigating examination process
- Maximizing protection scope
- Professional claim drafting
- Translation and filing assistance

### Finding Registered Patent Attorneys

**Official Register:** Sakpatent maintains an official register of licensed patent attorneys.

**Access the Register:**
1. Visit: https://www.sakpatenti.gov.ge/en/patent_attorneys/
2. View "Register of Patent Attorneys of Georgia"
3. Based on Law of Georgia on "Patent Attorneys of Georgia"

**What the Register Contains:**
- Name of patent attorney
- Registration number
- Contact information
- Areas of specialization
- Languages spoken
- Firm affiliation

### Selecting a Patent Attorney

**Criteria to Consider:**
- Experience with medical devices (for your transfemoral device)
- Experience with utility models vs. patents
- Language capabilities (English, Georgian)
- Fee structure and costs
- Location (Tbilisi, Mtskheta, or can work remotely)
- Client references
- Technical background
- Communication style

**Questions to Ask:**
1. How many utility model applications have you filed?
2. What is your success rate with Sakpatent examinations?
3. Do you have experience with medical device IP?
4. What are your fees for utility model filing?
5. Can you handle translations and all paperwork?
6. How do you charge - flat fee or hourly?
7. Can you assist with international filing (PCT)?
8. What is your typical timeline?

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### General Questions

**Q1: What is the difference between a patent and a utility model in Georgia?**
A: A utility model provides 10 years of protection with faster examination (1 month) and lower costs, but requires only 1 independent claim and local novelty. A patent provides 20 years of protection, requires full worldwide novelty examination, and allows multiple independent claims.

**Q2: Can I file in English or must I use Georgian?**
A: Application forms must be in Georgian. Technical documents can be filed in any language initially, but you must provide certified Georgian translation within 2 months.

**Q3: Can I file myself or do I need a patent attorney?**
A: Georgian residents can file themselves. Non-residents MUST use a registered Georgian patent attorney.

**Q4: How much does it cost to file a utility model?**
A: Basic costs: $100 (filing) + $150 (examination) + $150 (registration) = $400 standard. With e-filing (20% discount): $320. With inventor discount (70%): $120. See detailed fee section above.

**Q5: How long does the process take?**
A: Approximately 1 year from filing to registration, significantly faster than patents (which can take 2-3 years).

**Q6: Can I file online?**
A: Yes! Sakpatent has a full e-filing system at https://online.sakpatenti.gov.ge/ with 20% fee discount for online filing.

### Technical Questions

**Q7: Can I file for both a utility model and a patent for the same invention?**
A: Georgian law allows this strategy. You can file a utility model for faster protection while preparing a comprehensive patent application. However, be aware of novelty implications.

**Q8: What happens if I publicly disclose my invention before filing?**
A: Georgia has a 12-month grace period. You can disclose your invention and still file within 12 months without losing novelty. However, this only applies in Georgia - many other countries do not have grace periods.

**Q9: What can be protected as a utility model?**
A: Products (devices, apparatus), processes, substances, biological materials, and methods. Your transfemoral device would likely qualify.

**Q10: How many claims can a utility model have?**
A: Utility models must have exactly 1 independent claim. You can have multiple dependent claims that refer to the independent claim.

**Q11: What does "local novelty" mean?**
A: The examiner only checks if your utility model is new compared to existing Georgian patents and published applications. They don't search worldwide databases (unlike patent examination).

**Q12: Can methods/processes be protected as utility models?**
A: Yes, Georgian law allows process and method protection under utility models.

### Filing and Procedure Questions

**Q13: What happens if I miss a deadline?**
A: Most deadlines (like responding to office actions) cannot be extended. Missing a deadline usually results in proceedings termination. However, some deadlines may be extendable with additional fees.

**Q14: Can I amend my application after filing?**
A: Yes, but amendments cannot add new matter. You can clarify, narrow, or correct errors, but cannot expand the original disclosure.

**Q15: What if the examiner rejects my application?**
A: You can:
- Respond with arguments and amendments within 2 months
- Request reconsideration with new evidence
- Appeal the decision
- Abandon and refile with changes

**Q16: Do I need to conduct a patent search before filing?**
A: Not required, but recommended. Sakpatent offers preliminary patent search services. This helps assess novelty and avoid wasting fees on non-novel inventions.

**Q17: Can I file a PCT application and enter Georgian national phase?**
A: Yes! Georgia is a PCT member since 1991. You have 31 months from priority date to enter national phase. You get 50% fee reduction if ISR and Written Opinion were issued.

**Q18: What is the filing date?**
A: The date Sakpatent receives your complete application (or date documents are complete). This is crucial for establishing priority.

### Cost and Fee Questions

**Q19: What discounts are available?**
A:
- 20% for online e-filing (automatic)
- 70% for individual inventors, universities, research institutions
- 90% for students, school children, retirees
- 50% for PCT applications with ISR and Written Opinion

**Q20: Can I apply for a fee reduction?**
A: Yes, submit supporting documentation (student ID, retirement certificate, university affiliation letter) within 2 weeks of filing.

**Q21: When do I need to pay renewal fees?**
A: Annual renewal fees start from year 3. You must pay before each anniversary of the filing date to keep the utility model in force.

**Q22: What happens if I don't pay renewal fees?**
A: Your utility model lapses and protection ends. There may be a grace period with late fees, but protection can be permanently lost.

### International and Priority Questions

**Q23: If I file in Georgia, am I protected in other countries?**
A: No. IP protection is territorial. A Georgian utility model only protects you in Georgia. For other countries, you must file separate applications or use international systems (PCT).

**Q24: Can I claim priority from an earlier application?**
A: Yes, if you filed in another Paris Convention country within 12 months, you can claim priority. You must submit the priority document within 3 months.

**Q25: Can I extend my utility model protection beyond 10 years?**
A: No, utility models in Georgia have a fixed 10-year term. If you need longer protection, consider filing a patent (20 years).

**Q26: Does Georgia recognize European patents?**
A: Yes! Georgia has a European patent validation framework. European patents can be validated in Georgia.

### Post-Registration Questions

**Q27: What rights does a utility model give me?**
A: Exclusive rights to make, use, sell, import, and license your invention in Georgia for 10 years. You can prevent others from using your protected technology.

**Q28: How do I enforce my utility model rights?**
A: Through Georgian courts. You can sue infringers for damages and seek injunctions. Consider consulting an IP attorney for enforcement.

**Q29: Can I license or sell my utility model?**
A: Yes! You can license (allow others to use it for a fee) or assign (sell) your utility model rights. Transfers must be recorded with Sakpatent.

**Q30: What if someone infringes my utility model?**
A: You can:
- Send a cease and desist letter
- Negotiate a license agreement
- File a lawsuit for infringement
- Seek damages and injunctions
- Report to Georgian customs (to stop imports)

### For Medical Device Inventors

**Q31: Are medical devices patentable as utility models in Georgia?**
A: Yes! Medical devices and prosthetics (like your transfemoral device) can be protected as utility models if they meet novelty and industrial applicability requirements.

**Q32: Should I file a utility model or patent for my medical device?**
A: Consider:
- **Utility Model:** If it's an incremental improvement, you need faster protection, or have limited budget
- **Patent:** If it's a major innovation, you need long-term protection, or plan international expansion
- **Both:** File utility model first for fast protection, then file comprehensive patent

**Q33: Do I need clinical trial data to file?**
A: No. Utility models protect the technical design and functionality. Clinical data, safety testing, and regulatory approval are separate from IP protection.

**Q34: Can I file before my device is fully developed?**
A: Yes, but your application must fully describe how to make and use the invention. You can file based on prototypes or detailed designs.

**Q35: Does a utility model help with regulatory approval?**
A: Utility model registration is separate from regulatory approval (CE marking, FDA, etc.). However, having IP protection can be valuable when seeking regulatory approval and partnerships.

### Practical Tips

**Q36: What are common mistakes to avoid?**
A:
- Filing without proper novelty search
- Using unclear or overly broad language
- Missing deadlines for responses
- Not using professional translation
- Forgetting to pay fees on time
- Disclosing invention publicly before filing (outside grace period)
- Filing in wrong category (patent vs. utility model)
- Not keeping proper records

**Q37: How do I maximize my chances of success?**
A:
- Conduct thorough prior art search
- Use clear, precise technical language
- Include detailed drawings and examples
- Draft claims carefully (remember: only 1 independent claim)
- Respond promptly to office actions
- Consider hiring experienced patent attorney
- Keep detailed invention records
- File as soon as invention is complete

**Q38: Can I make changes after submitting online?**
A: Minor corrections may be possible before formal examination. Major changes require amendments in response to office actions or withdrawal and refiling.

**Q39: What if my device has multiple innovative features?**
A: You can:
- File one utility model with 1 independent claim covering the main feature
- File multiple utility model applications (one for each feature)
- File a patent with multiple independent claims
- File both utility model (for quick protection of main feature) and patent (for comprehensive protection)

**Q40: Where can I get help if I'm stuck?**
A:
- Contact Sakpatent: info@sakpatenti.org.ge or +995 322 25 25 33
- Hire a registered Georgian patent attorney
- Consult with IP law firms
- Use WIPO resources for PCT applications
- Join inventor organizations in Georgia
- Contact technology transfer offices (if affiliated with university)
