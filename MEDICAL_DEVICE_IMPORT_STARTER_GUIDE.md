## Executive Summary: What You Need to Know First

### Critical Success Factors

Before investing any money, understand these fundamental truths:

| Factor | Reality Check |
|--------|---------------|
| **Minimum Investment** | $25,000 - $75,000 for a meaningful first batch |
| **Time to First Sale** | 3-6 months (registration + shipping + distribution setup) |
| **Shelf Life Constraint** | 2-3 years maximum; you need fast turnover |
| **Regulatory Barrier** | CE Mark or FDA approval required for Georgia |
| **Insurance Requirement** | Product liability insurance is mandatory |
| **Storage Requirement** | Climate-controlled storage (15-30°C typical) |

### The "Reality Check" Questions

Ask yourself these questions before proceeding:

1. **Do you have hospital/clinic relationships?** Without pre-existing buyer relationships, you'll struggle to sell specialized medical devices.

2. **Can you provide technical support?** Interventional cardiologists expect product training and procedural support.

3. **Do you have $50,000+ in working capital?** Between inventory, registration, insurance, and operations, undercapitalization is the #1 killer.

4. **Can you wait 6+ months for revenue?** Registration, shipping, and sales cycles take time.

5. **Do you have a medical background or advisor?** Understanding the clinical context is essential.

## Product Shelf Life & Storage Requirements

### Shelf Life by Product Category

| Product Type | Typical Shelf Life | Critical Notes |
|--------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Drug Coated Balloons (DCB)** | 2 years | Drug coating stability is limiting factor |
| **PTCA Balloon Catheters** | 2-3 years | Polymer aging affects performance |
| **Cutting Balloons** | 2-3 years | Blade integrity maintained |
| **Scoring Balloons** | 2-3 years | Wire integrity maintained |
| **Coronary Stents (DES)** | 2-3 years | Drug coating stability |
| **Guidewires** | 3-5 years | Longer shelf life |
| **Guide Extension Catheters** | 2-3 years | Polymer considerations |

### Shelf Life Business Implications

#### The 18-Month Rule
> **Best Practice:** Never import products with less than 18 months remaining shelf life. This gives you:
> - 6 months for shipping + registration + distribution setup
> - 12 months to sell the inventory

#### Inventory Turnover Calculation

```
Example: You import 50 Drug Eluting Balloons
- Shelf life remaining at import: 24 months
- Setup time: 4 months
- Usable selling time: 20 months
- Minimum monthly sales needed: 50 ÷ 20 = 2.5 units/month

If you can't sell 3+ units per month, you risk expiration losses.
```

### Storage Requirements

#### Temperature Requirements

| Product | Storage Temperature | Humidity | Light |
|---------|--------------------| ---------|-------|
| Most balloon catheters | 15°C - 30°C (59°F - 86°F) | Protected from humidity | Away from direct sunlight |
| Drug coated balloons | 15°C - 25°C (59°F - 77°F) | <60% RH recommended | Protected from light |
| Guidewires | Room temperature | Standard | Standard |

#### Storage Facility Requirements

1. **Climate Control**
   - Temperature monitoring and logging
   - Air conditioning for hot months
   - Heating for cold months (if applicable)

2. **Organization**
   - First-In-First-Out (FIFO) inventory management
   - Clear expiration date labeling
   - Separate areas for different product categories

3. **Security**
   - Controlled access
   - Inventory tracking system
   - Theft prevention

4. **Documentation**
   - Temperature logs (daily)
   - Inventory records
   - Lot/batch traceability

### Warning Signs During Storage

| Issue | Action |
|-------|--------|
| Package damage | Quarantine immediately; do not sell |
| Sterility seal broken | Dispose; cannot be used |
| Temperature excursion | Contact manufacturer for guidance |
| Approaching expiration | Discount or return if possible |

## Legal & Regulatory Requirements

### Georgia's Regulatory Framework

#### Regulatory Authority

**RAMA (State Regulation Agency of Medical Activities)**
- Under: Ministry of Health
- Function: Medical device registration and market surveillance

#### Accepted Certifications

| Certification | Acceptance Status | Notes |
|---------------|-------------------|-------|
| **CE Mark (EU MDR)** | ✅ Fully Accepted | Preferred certification |
| **FDA Approval** | ✅ Fully Accepted | US market clearance |
| **NMPA (China)** | ⚠️ May need additional | Supplement with EU guidelines |
| **ISO 13485** | ✅ Required | Quality management system |

#### ESC Guidelines Requirement

Products mentioned in **European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines** have enhanced acceptance. Verify your chosen products appear in relevant clinical guidelines.

### Registration Process for Georgia

#### Step 1: Gather Documentation

| Document | Source | Purpose |
|----------|--------|---------|
| CE Certificate | Manufacturer | Proves EU approval |
| Declaration of Conformity | Manufacturer | Legal declaration |
| ISO 13485 Certificate | Manufacturer | Quality system proof |
| Technical File/Dossier | Manufacturer | Product specifications |
| Instructions for Use (IFU) | Manufacturer | Usage documentation |
| Clinical data/studies | Manufacturer | Safety evidence |
| Free Sale Certificate | Manufacturer's country | Market authorization proof |
| Authorization letter | Manufacturer | Grants you import rights |

#### Step 2: Prepare Georgian Documentation

| Document | Your Responsibility |
|----------|---------------------|
| Georgian translation of labels | Mandatory |
| Georgian translation of IFU | Mandatory |
| Import license application | Submit to RAMA |
| Authorized representative declaration | If required |
| Vigilance system description | Post-market surveillance |

#### Step 3: Submit Registration

1. Submit complete dossier to RAMA
2. Pay registration fees
3. Await review (typically 30-90 days)
4. Respond to any queries
5. Receive registration certificate

#### Step 4: Maintain Compliance

- Renew registration every 5 years
- Report adverse events immediately
- Maintain traceability records
- Cooperate with market surveillance

1. General business registration

### Distributor Agreement Essentials

When signing with a manufacturer, ensure these terms:

#### Must-Have Clauses

| Clause | Why It Matters |
|--------|----------------|
| **Territory definition** | Confirms Georgia exclusivity |
| **Minimum purchase obligations** | Know your commitments |
| **Pricing and payment terms** | Cash flow planning |
| **Warranty terms** | Defective product handling |
| **Product liability allocation** | Who pays for claims |
| **Training provision** | Technical support |
| **Marketing support** | Promotional materials |
| **Return policy** | Unsold/expired inventory |
| **Termination conditions** | Exit strategy |

#### Red Flags in Contracts

- Unrealistic minimum order quantities
- No product liability coverage by manufacturer
- No provision for defective product returns
- Automatic renewal without notice period
- Exclusive obligations without reciprocal benefits

## Customs, Duties & Tariffs for Georgia

### Tariff Structure

Georgia has one of the most liberal import regimes:

| Category | Rate |
|----------|------|
| **Import Duty** | 0% for most medical devices |
| **VAT** | 18% (on CIF value + duty) |

> **Good News:** Nearly 90% of goods enter Georgia duty-free, and medical devices typically fall into the 0% duty category.

### HS Code Classification

#### Common HS Codes for Your Products

| Product | HS Code | Description |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| Balloon catheters | **9018.39** | Catheters, cannulae and the like |
| Cardiac catheters | **9018.39.20** | Cardiac catheters specifically |
| Coronary stents | **9021.90.90** | Other orthopedic appliances |
| Guidewires | **9018.39** | Medical instruments |

> **IMPORTANT:** Incorrect HS code classification can result in:
> - Delayed customs clearance
> - Unexpected duties
> - Legal penalties
> - Shipment detention

#### How to Verify HS Codes

1. Ask manufacturer for their export HS code
2. Consult Georgia's Revenue Service
3. Use customs broker assistance
4. Reference CROSS database for precedents

### Customs Clearance Process

#### Required Documents for Customs

| Document | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| Commercial Invoice | Value declaration |
| Packing List | Contents verification |
| Bill of Lading/Airway Bill | Shipping proof |
| Certificate of Origin | Country verification |
| CE Certificate copy | Regulatory compliance |
| Proforma Invoice | If applicable |
| Import License | Regulatory authorization |

#### Customs Fees

| Shipment Value | Customs Service Fee |
|----------------|---------------------|
| < 300 GEL | Minimal fee |
| 300 - 3,000 GEL | 20 GEL |
| 3,000 - 10,000 GEL | 100 GEL |
| > 10,000 GEL | Scaled fee |

### Calculation Example

```
Example: Importing $20,000 worth of balloon catheters

CIF Value (Cost + Insurance + Freight): $20,000
Import Duty (0%): $0
Subtotal: $20,000
VAT (18%): $3,600
Customs fees: ~$100
TOTAL LANDED COST: $23,700
```

### Working with a Customs Broker

**Strongly Recommended for First-Time Importers**

A customs broker provides:
- Correct HS code classification
- Documentation preparation
- Customs negotiation
- Faster clearance
- Compliance assurance

**Cost:** Typically $150-$500 per shipment

## Insurance Requirements

### Types of Insurance Needed

#### 1. Product Liability Insurance (MANDATORY)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Purpose** | Covers claims from product defects causing injury |
| **Coverage Amount** | Minimum $1,000,000 per occurrence |
| **Annual Cost** | $3,000 - $10,000 (based on revenue, risk class) |
| **Who Requires It** | Hospitals, manufacturers, regulators |

#### Why You Need Product Liability Insurance

1. **Hospitals require it** - They won't buy from uninsured distributors
2. **Legal protection** - Medical devices can cause serious harm
3. **Manufacturer requirement** - Most distributor agreements mandate it
4. **Regulatory expectation** - EU MDR explicitly mentions it

#### 2. Cargo/Transit Insurance

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Purpose** | Covers goods during shipping |
| **Coverage** | 110% of shipment value (typical) |
| **Cost** | 0.5% - 2% of shipment value |

#### 3. General Business Insurance

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Purpose** | Office, warehouse, general liability |
| **Coverage** | Property damage, premises liability |
| **Cost** | $1,000 - $3,000 annually |

### Insurance Cost Estimates

| Insurance Type | Annual Cost Range | Priority |
|---------------|-------------------|----------|
| Product Liability | $3,000 - $10,000 | CRITICAL |
| Cargo/Transit | $500 - $2,000 | HIGH |
| General Business | $1,000 - $3,000 | MEDIUM |
| **TOTAL** | **$4,500 - $15,000** | |

### Getting Insurance Quotes

Contact these types of insurers:
- Specialized medical device insurers
- International cargo insurers
- Local business insurance providers

**Tip:** Start the insurance process early - approval can take 2-4 weeks.

## Common Rookie Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake #1: Inadequate Capital Planning

**The Error:** Underestimating total costs; running out of money before first sale

**The Reality:**
- Hidden costs always emerge
- Registration takes longer than expected
- First sales take longer than expected

**The Solution:**
- Have 50% more capital than your minimum calculation
- Plan for 6 months of zero revenue
- Keep emergency reserve

---

### Mistake #2: Ignoring Shelf Life When Ordering

**The Error:** Ordering too much inventory relative to sales capacity

**The Consequence:**
- Products expire before sale
- Total loss on expired inventory
- Cash flow devastation

**The Solution:**
- Start with small orders (50-100 units per category)
- Verify remaining shelf life before shipping
- Demand minimum 18+ months shelf life at delivery
- Build sales channels before scaling inventory

---

### Mistake #3: Wrong HS Code Declaration

**The Error:** Using incorrect harmonized system codes on customs declarations

**The Consequences:**
- Shipment detention at customs
- Potential penalties
- Delays of weeks/months
- Unexpected duty payments

**The Solution:**
- Use a customs broker for first shipments
- Get HS code confirmation from manufacturer
- Verify with Georgian customs before shipping

---

### Mistake #4: Incomplete Documentation

**The Error:** Missing certificates, translations, or authorizations

**The Consequences:**
- Registration refusal
- Customs detention
- Months of delays
- Products stuck at port incurring fees

**The Solution:**
- Create document checklist
- Verify EVERY document before shipping
- Have manufacturer confirm documentation is complete
- Keep certified copies of everything

---

### Mistake #5: No Pre-Established Customer Base

**The Error:** Importing products before having buyers lined up

**The Reality:**
- Interventional cardiologists are loyal to brands
- Hospitals have procurement processes
- Convincing new customers takes months

**The Solution:**
- Talk to hospitals BEFORE importing
- Get letters of intent or pre-orders
- Understand hospital procurement cycles
- Build relationships with cardiologists first

---

### Mistake #6: Skipping Product Liability Insurance

**The Error:** Trying to save money by not getting insurance

**The Consequences:**
- Hospitals refuse to buy
- One lawsuit can bankrupt you
- Personal assets at risk
- Manufacturer may terminate agreement

**The Solution:**
- Budget for insurance from day one
- Get quotes before committing to import
- Consider it a non-negotiable cost of business

---

### Mistake #7: Not Understanding Clinical Context

**The Error:** Treating medical devices like regular consumer products

**The Reality:**
- Cardiologists need product training
- Each device has specific indications
- Wrong device selection can harm patients
- Technical support is expected

**The Solution:**
- Get trained on products you sell
- Attend manufacturer training programs
- Hire medical sales professionals
- Partner with clinical consultants

---

### Mistake #8: Ignoring Post-Market Obligations

**The Error:** Thinking registration is the end of compliance

**The Obligations:**
- Adverse event reporting
- Complaint handling
- Product recalls (if needed)
- Vigilance system maintenance

**The Consequences:**
- Registration cancellation
- Legal penalties
- Criminal liability in severe cases

**The Solution:**
- Establish complaint handling procedures
- Know reporting requirements
- Keep contact with manufacturer for recalls
- Document everything

---

### Mistake #9: Poor Storage Conditions

**The Error:** Storing temperature-sensitive devices improperly

**The Consequences:**
- Product degradation
- Sterility compromise
- Cannot sell compromised products
- Total inventory loss

**The Solution:**
- Invest in climate-controlled storage
- Monitor and log temperature daily
- Train staff on handling procedures
- Inspect packages upon receipt

---

### Mistake #10: Choosing Price Over Quality

**The Error:** Selecting cheapest supplier without quality verification

**The Consequences:**
- Product failures
- Physician complaints
- Reputation damage
- Liability exposure

**The Solution:**
- Verify all certifications independently
- Request reference customers
- Start with sample evaluation
- Check for clinical trial data

## Risk Assessment Matrix

### Risk Identification and Mitigation

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation Strategy |
|------|------------|--------|---------------------|
| **Regulatory rejection** | Medium | High | Thorough documentation, use consultant |
| **Products expire before sale** | Medium | High | Small initial orders, pre-sell before importing |
| **Customs detention** | Low | Medium | Use customs broker, correct documentation |
| **Product quality issues** | Low | High | Verify certifications, sample testing |
| **Manufacturer relationship failure** | Low | High | Multiple supplier relationships |
| **Customer non-payment** | Medium | Medium | Credit checks, payment terms |
| **Storage failure** | Low | High | Climate control, monitoring, insurance |
| **Price competition** | Medium | Medium | Value-added services, differentiation |
| **Currency fluctuation** | Medium | Medium | Price adjustment clauses |
| **Product recall** | Very Low | Very High | Insurance, manufacturer agreement |

### Contingency Planning

**If products don't sell:**
1. Negotiate return to manufacturer
2. Discount to move inventory before expiration
3. Expand geographic reach (neighboring countries)
4. Partner with larger distributor

**If registration is rejected:**
1. Address deficiencies and reapply
2. Consider alternative products with better documentation
3. Seek different certification route

**If manufacturer fails:**
1. Have secondary supplier relationship
2. Maintain buffer inventory
3. Document all agreements for legal recourse
