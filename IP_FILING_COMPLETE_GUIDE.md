## Understanding the Patent System

### What is a Patent?

A patent is a government-granted right that gives you the exclusive ability to **make, use, sell, and import** your invention for **20 years** from the filing date. In exchange, you must publicly disclose how your invention works.

### Types of Patent Applications

#### 1. Provisional Patent Application (PPA)
- **Purpose:** Placeholder that establishes your priority date
- **Duration:** 12 months (cannot be extended)
- **Examination:** NOT examined by USPTO
- **Result:** Does NOT become a patent by itself
- **Cost:** $65 (micro entity) to $325 (large entity)
- **Requirements:** Less formal; no claims required

#### 2. Non-Provisional Patent Application (NPA)
- **Purpose:** The "real" application that gets examined
- **Duration:** Examined over 18-36 months typically
- **Examination:** Full examination by USPTO examiner
- **Result:** Can become an issued patent
- **Cost:** $1,000+ in fees alone (plus attorney costs if used)
- **Requirements:** Formal claims, drawings, full specification

#### 3. PCT International Application
- **Purpose:** Single application that preserves rights in 150+ countries
- **Duration:** 30 months from priority date to enter national phase
- **Examination:** International search and optional examination
- **Result:** Must enter "national phase" in each desired country
- **Cost:** ~$3,000-$5,000 for international phase

### Patent Pending vs. Issued Patent

| Aspect | Patent Pending | Issued Patent |
|--------|----------------|---------------|
| **Status** | Application filed, not yet granted | Patent granted and active |
| **Rights** | Cannot sue for infringement yet | Full enforcement rights |
| **Duration** | Until patent issues or application abandoned | 20 years from filing |
| **Marking** | Can mark products "Patent Pending" | Must mark with patent number |
| **Value** | Shows innovation, deters copying | Full legal protection |

## Recommended Filing Strategy

### Phase 1: US Provisional Patent Application (IMMEDIATE)

**Goal:** Establish priority date and obtain "Patent Pending" status

**Timeline:** File as soon as possible, ideally BEFORE any public disclosure

**Steps:**
1. Prepare description of your invention
2. Create drawings/diagrams
3. File online via USPTO Patent Center
4. Receive filing receipt = "Patent Pending"

**Cost:** $65 (micro entity) | $130 (small entity) | $325 (large entity)

### Phase 2: PCT International Application (Within 12 months)

**Goal:** Preserve international rights with single filing

**Timeline:** Must file within 12 months of provisional filing date

**Steps:**
1. Prepare formal application with claims
2. File via WIPO ePCT system or through USPTO as receiving office
3. Receive international search report
4. Optional: Request preliminary examination

**Cost:** $3,000-$5,000 (includes filing, search, and transmittal fees)

### Phase 3: National Phase Entry (Within 30 months)

**Goal:** Convert PCT application into actual patents in desired countries

**Timeline:** Must enter national phase within 30 months of original priority date

**Target Markets:**
- **United States:** Enter via USPTO
- **Europe:** Enter via EPO (covers 39 countries with single application)

**Cost:** Varies significantly by country ($2,000-$10,000+ per country)

## US Patent Filing - Step by Step

### Creating Your USPTO Account

#### Step 1: Create USPTO.gov Account

1. Go to: **https://account.uspto.gov/**
2. Click "Create Account"
3. Enter your email address
4. Create a password
5. Verify your email

#### Step 2: Verify Your Identity

**Option A: Online via ID.me (Recommended - ~30 minutes)**
1. Go to: **https://www.id.me/**
2. Create ID.me account
3. Verify identity using:
   - Driver's license or passport
   - Selfie verification
   - Phone verification
4. Link ID.me to your USPTO account

**Option B: Paper Verification (5-10 business days)**
1. Download form from USPTO website
2. Complete and notarize
3. Mail to USPTO
4. Wait for confirmation

#### Step 3: Self-Enroll in Patent Center

1. Go to: **https://patentcenter.uspto.gov/**
2. Sign in with USPTO.gov account
3. Complete self-enrollment process
4. You're now ready to file!

### Filing a Provisional Patent Application

#### Required Documents

| Document | Description | Required? |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Cover Sheet (SB/16) | Basic application information | Yes |
| Specification | Written description of invention | Yes |
| Drawings | Visual representations | Recommended |
| Inventor Declaration | Not required for provisional | No |
| Claims | Not required for provisional | No |
| Fees | Filing fee payment | Yes |

#### Step-by-Step Filing Process

**Step 1: Prepare Your Documents**

Create the following in PDF format:

**A. Cover Sheet**
- Download form SB/16 from: https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/sb0016_2.pdf
- Fill in:
  - Invention title
  - Inventor name(s) and address(es)
  - Correspondence address
  - Entity status (micro/small/large)

**B. Specification (Written Description)**

Your specification should include:

```
TITLE OF THE INVENTION
[Your device name]

CROSS-REFERENCE TO RELATED APPLICATIONS
[Leave blank for first filing or reference any related applications]

FIELD OF THE INVENTION
[General technical field - e.g., "medical devices for vascular access"]

BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION
[Problem being solved, limitations of existing solutions]

SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION
[Brief overview of your solution]

DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION
[Comprehensive explanation including:]
- Components and their functions
- How components interact
- Materials used
- Dimensions (if relevant)
- Method of use
- Alternative embodiments
- Advantages over prior art

BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE DRAWINGS
[List each figure and what it shows]

REFERENCE TO DRAWINGS
Figure 1 - [Description]
Figure 2 - [Description]
[etc.]
```

**C. Drawings**
- Black and white line drawings preferred
- Clear labels with reference numbers
- Multiple views (perspective, cross-section, exploded)
- Number each figure
- Format: 8.5" x 11" or A4, portrait orientation

**Step 2: Access Patent Center**

1. Go to: **https://patentcenter.uspto.gov/**
2. Click "Sign In"
3. Log in with your USPTO.gov credentials

**Step 3: Start New Application**

1. Click "New Submission"
2. Select "Patent"
3. Select "Provisional Application"
4. Click "Continue"

**Step 4: Enter Application Data**

1. **Application Data:**
   - Enter title of invention
   - Select entity status:
     - **Micro Entity** (80% fee reduction) - if you qualify
     - **Small Entity** (60% fee reduction) - if you qualify
     - **Large Entity** (full fees)

2. **Inventor Information:**
   - Enter all inventor names
   - Include addresses
   - Designate correspondence address

**Step 5: Upload Documents**

1. Click "Attach Documents"
2. Upload your PDF files:
   - Specification
   - Drawings
   - Cover Sheet (SB/16)
3. Verify all documents uploaded correctly

**Step 6: Calculate and Pay Fees**

1. System will calculate fees based on:
   - Entity status
   - Number of pages (extra fee if >100 pages)
2. **2025 Provisional Filing Fees:**
   - Micro Entity: **$65**
   - Small Entity: **$130**
   - Large Entity: **$325**
3. Pay via:
   - Credit card
   - USPTO deposit account
   - Electronic funds transfer

**Step 7: Submit Application**

1. Review all information
2. Click "Submit"
3. Receive electronic acknowledgment receipt
4. **Save your application number** - this is your proof of "Patent Pending"

### Qualifying for Micro Entity Status (80% Fee Reduction)

To qualify, you must meet ALL of the following:

#### Gross Income Basis (Most Common)

| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| Small Entity Status | Must first qualify as small entity |
| Application Limit | Named as inventor on 4 or fewer previously filed US patent applications |
| Income Limit | Gross income below $251,190 (2025 threshold) |
| Assignment | Not assigned/obligated to assign to entity exceeding income limit |

#### How to Claim Micro Entity Status

1. Download Form SB/15A: https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/sb0015a.pdf
2. Complete and sign the certification
3. Submit with your application
4. **Important:** Re-certify each time you pay fees

#### Penalties for False Claims

- Must pay difference at full rate
- Administrative penalties possible
- Potential exclusion from micro/small entity program

### Filing a Non-Provisional Patent Application

If you're filing non-provisional directly (or converting from provisional):

#### Additional Required Documents

## International Patent Filing (PCT)

### What is the PCT?

The Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) is an international agreement that allows you to file a single "international" patent application that preserves your rights in over 150 countries. It does NOT grant an international patent (there is no such thing), but it:

- Establishes a single filing date in all member countries
- Provides an international search report
- Delays national filing costs by up to 30 months
- Simplifies the international filing process

### PCT Timeline

```

(Provisional filing)                │                      │                      │

       │                            │                      │                      │
       └────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┘
                                PATENT PENDING MAINTAINED THROUGHOUT
```

### Filing a PCT Application

#### Option A: File Through WIPO Directly (ePCT)

**Step 1: Start New Application**
1. Log into ePCT
2. Click "Create New International Application"
3. Select your Receiving Office (RO)
   - Can use International Bureau (RO/IB) as receiving office
   - Or use USPTO (RO/US) if US applicant

**Step 2: Complete Application Form (PCT/RO/101)**

The system will guide you through:
- Applicant information
- Inventor information
- Priority claims (your provisional application)
- Title and abstract
- Designation of states (automatic for all PCT members)

**Step 3: Upload Documents**
- Description
- Claims
- Abstract
- Drawings
- Priority document (if not using DAS)

**Step 4: Pay Fees**

#### Option B: File Through USPTO as Receiving Office

1. Go to USPTO Patent Center: **https://patentcenter.uspto.gov/**
2. Start new submission
3. Select "PCT International Application"
4. Follow similar process as above
5. Pay fees in USD

   - Use Digital Access Service (DAS) for priority documents
   - Avoid certified copy fees

## Freedom to Operate (FTO) Analysis

### What is FTO?

**Freedom to Operate (FTO)** means you can manufacture, sell, and use your product without infringing someone else's patent rights.

**CRITICAL DISTINCTION:**
- **Patentability** = Can YOU get a patent?
- **FTO** = Can you SELL without infringing OTHERS' patents?

You can have a patentable invention but still infringe someone else's patent!

### Why FTO is Critical for Medical Devices

1. **High Development Costs** - Medical devices require significant R&D investment
2. **Regulatory Investment** - FDA/CE approval is expensive
3. **Investor Requirements** - Investors demand FTO clearance
4. **Litigation Risk** - Patent lawsuits in medical field are common and expensive
5. **Market Entry** - Blocking patents can prevent market entry

### How to Conduct FTO Analysis

#### Step 1: Define Your Product

Document every component and feature:
- Physical components
- Materials used
- Manufacturing methods
- Methods of use
- Software (if applicable)

#### Step 2: Define Geographic Scope

Focus on markets where you will:
- Manufacture
- Sell
- Import
- Use

**For your case:** US and EU

#### Step 3: Search for Relevant Patents

**Free Search Tools:**

| Database | URL | Coverage |
|----------|-----|----------|
| USPTO Patent Public Search | https://ppubs.uspto.gov/pubsweb/ | US patents and applications |
| Google Patents | https://patents.google.com/ | Global patents |
| Espacenet | https://worldwide.espacenet.com/ | 140M+ documents |
| WIPO PATENTSCOPE | https://patentscope.wipo.int/ | PCT and national collections |

**Search Strategy:**
1. **Keyword Search** - Use technical terms describing your invention
2. **Classification Search** - Use IPC/CPC codes for medical devices
3. **Assignee Search** - Search known competitors
4. **Citation Search** - Follow citation chains from relevant patents

**Relevant Patent Classifications for Medical Devices:**
- A61B - Diagnosis; Surgery; Identification
- A61F - Filters implantable into blood vessels; Prostheses
- A61M - Devices for introducing media into, or onto, the body

#### Step 4: Analyze Patent Claims

**Only the CLAIMS define what's protected!**

For each potentially relevant patent:
1. Read all independent claims
2. Identify claim elements
3. Compare to your product feature-by-feature
4. Determine if your product has ALL elements of any claim

**Claim Analysis Matrix:**

| Claim Element | Your Product Has? | Notes |
|---------------|-------------------|-------|
| Element A | Yes/No/Modified | |
| Element B | Yes/No/Modified | |
| Element C | Yes/No/Modified | |

If your product has ALL elements of a claim = potential infringement

#### Step 5: Check Patent Status

For each potentially blocking patent, verify:
- Is it still in force (not expired)?
- Has it been maintained (fees paid)?
- Is it valid in your target countries?
- Has it been challenged or invalidated?

**Check patent status:**
- USPTO: https://portal.uspto.gov/pair/PublicPair
- EPO: https://register.epo.org/

#### Step 6: Document Everything

Create a written record of:
- Search strategy and terms used
- Databases searched
- Patents identified and analyzed
- Conclusions for each patent
- Date of analysis

This demonstrates "due diligence" if ever challenged.

### FTO Analysis Options

| Option | Cost | Thoroughness | Recommendation |
|--------|------|--------------|----------------|
| Self-Conducted | Free | Low-Medium | Good for initial screening |
| Professional Search | $2,000-$5,000 | Medium-High | Recommended for key patents |
| Full FTO Opinion | $10,000-$50,000+ | High | Required for investors/major decisions |

### What if You Find Blocking Patents?

**Options:**
1. **Design Around** - Modify your design to avoid infringement
2. **License** - Negotiate license with patent owner
3. **Challenge Validity** - If patent is weak, challenge it
4. **Acquire** - Purchase the patent or company
5. **Wait** - If patent expires soon
6. **Accept Risk** - Document reasoning (not recommended)


## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake #1: Public Disclosure Before Filing

**The Problem:** Any public disclosure before filing destroys patent rights in most countries.

**What Counts as Disclosure:**
- Publishing papers or articles
- Presenting at conferences
- Posting on social media
- Showing to investors (without NDA)
- Crowdfunding campaigns
- Trade shows
- Sales or offers for sale

**US Grace Period:** US allows 1-year grace period after disclosure, BUT international rights are lost immediately.

**Solution:** ALWAYS file provisional before any disclosure.

### Mistake #2: Inadequate Provisional Application

**The Problem:** Provisional must support future claims to get priority benefit.

**Common Issues:**
- Too brief description
- Missing alternative embodiments
- No drawings or poor drawings
- Fails to describe how to make and use

**Solution:** Write provisional as if it were a full application.

### Mistake #3: Missing the 12-Month PCT Deadline

**The Problem:** PCT must be filed within 12 months of provisional - NO EXTENSIONS.

**Solution:** Calendar the deadline immediately. File PCT by month 11 to allow buffer.

### Mistake #4: Skipping Prior Art Search

**The Problem:** You may file for something already patented or obvious.

**Consequences:**
- Wasted filing fees
- Potential infringement of others' patents
- Weak or invalid patent

**Solution:** Search before filing provisional. Refine based on findings.

### Mistake #5: Confusing Patentability with FTO

**The Problem:** Getting a patent does NOT mean you can sell your product.

**Example:** You patent an improvement, but the base technology is patented by someone else. You need THEIR permission to practice your improvement.

**Solution:** Do BOTH patentability AND FTO analysis.

### Mistake #6: Filing Overly Broad Claims

**The Problem:** Very broad claims get rejected; very narrow claims offer little protection.

**Solution:** File with claims at multiple levels of breadth (broad independent claims, narrower dependent claims).

### Mistake #7: Poor Documentation

**The Problem:** Can't prove when you invented; can't demonstrate due diligence.

**Solution:** Keep detailed lab notebooks with:
- Dated entries
- Detailed descriptions
- Sketches and drawings
- Witness signatures

### Mistake #8: Not Working with Professionals

**The Problem:** Patent law is complex. Mistakes can be costly and permanent.

**When to Hire:**
- Non-provisional applications
- Complex inventions
- PCT applications
- FTO opinions
- Prosecution (responding to USPTO)

**How to Save Costs:**
- File provisional yourself
- Do initial prior art search yourself
- Prepare detailed documentation before meeting attorney
- Use patent agents (cheaper than attorneys) for some tasks
